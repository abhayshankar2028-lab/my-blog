![** The Phantom Fringe: Kattalan Review

**](https://images.indianexpress.com/2026/05/Kattalan-movie-review-rating-Antony-Varghese-Pepe.jpg)

**

In the depths of the Malayalam cinema, a film has emerged, a thriller that promises to weave a web of suspense, but succumbs to the thrill of the reel. Kattalan, directed by Antony Varghese, is a film that feels like a series of reels, crafted to deceive the audience into believing it's a whole narrative. The story, a complex mix of revenge, betrayal, and redemption, is reduced to a series of disjointed moments, each one a fleeting glimpse into the lives of its characters.

The film's pacing is frenetic, like a speeding bullet, with each scene hurtling forward at breakneck speed, leaving the audience gasping for breath. But amidst the chaos, the characters remain static, their motivations and emotions reduced to simplistic archetypes. The result is a film that feels more like a montage of clichés than a genuine exploration of the human condition.

As the story careens from one twist to the next, the audience is left feeling dizzy and disoriented, like a spectator trapped in a kaleidoscope. The film's climax, a meandering and poorly constructed sequence, is the final nail in the coffin, a brutal reminder that Kattalan is just a series of reels, crafted to deceive and entertain.
