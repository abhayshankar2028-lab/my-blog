In the swirling vortex of the Indian Premier League, Punjab Kings embarked on a meteoric rise to favouritism, the mythical sultan of spells casting a mesmerizing spell on the cricketing world. But, like a fleeting summer breeze, the winds of change began to whisper doubts in the ears of their mighty colony. "Invasion" was the harbinger of their demise, a coup d'état orchestrated by the imperious Mumbai Indians. As the Kangra River of chaos flowed, the once-untouchable Kings were swept away by the undertow of underperformance, their magnificent edifice crumbling beneath the relentless onslaught of the more agile and cunning newcomers. The curtains closed on a season of promises, leaving the stunned audience to ponder the haunting question: what was it about the Kings' invincibility that made it so irresistible to the cricketing gods?
